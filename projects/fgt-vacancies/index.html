Vacancies in Fe3GeTe2

Fe-vacancy-driven noncollinear magnetism in a van der Waals ferromagnet monolayer

collaborators: Prof. Biplab Sanyal (Uppsala University), Prof. Phani Motamarri

Manuscript in preparation.

Fe3GeTe2 is a layered van der Waals ferromagnet that remains magnetic down to a single layer, making it a leading candidate for two-dimensional spintronics. Its magnetic properties are known to be sensitive to Fe deficiency, but a microscopic picture of how individual vacancies reshape the local magnetism has been missing. Answering this question requires large supercells together with a fully relativistic, noncollinear treatment of magnetism, a combination that has been computationally out of reach.

Using the noncollinear finite-element DFT and GPU algorithms developed during my Ph.D., we carry out large-scale, fully relativistic noncollinear DFT calculations of Fe vacancies in monolayer Fe3GeTe2. We examine:

  • which of the two inequivalent Fe sublattices vacancies prefer to form on;
  • how a vacancy reconstructs the magnetic moments of the surrounding Fe atoms and how far this disturbance extends;
  • the vacancy-induced canting of the spins away from the collinear ferromagnetic order;
  • how pairs of vacancies interact with each other.

These calculations connect the composition sensitivity of Fe3-xGeTe2 to a microscopic picture of defect formation and local magnetic order, and provide a reference for future studies of supported layers and heterostructures.
